# Milestone 7: the player's polish

A survey of what milestone 7 needs and a plan for splitting it into agent tasks, each checkable on its own. Surveyed on 2026-11-27 from `main` at `61d4e460`, the day the refactor round closed with its task 34 left to run ([the refactor](refactor.md#the-rounds-end)).

Milestones 4 to 6 and the Slippi ruleset made the game whole and exact. This one is about the person at the controller: plugging in what they own and having it work, reaching the settings without a keyboard, getting melee-hd onto their machine, and the presentation options players expect. Nothing here changes what the simulation does with a given input.

The milestone is done when:

- **Tier 1, input and usability:** a player picks which controller plays which port and rebinds any of them; box controllers (B0XX, HayBox, Frame1) play over USB with their exact coordinates, and a keyboard can play with a box layout; stock controllers on the adapter can have PhobGCC's processing; the settings are reachable from a controller and from Melee's own Options menu; the player knows whether the display runs variable refresh and when a frame reached the screen; the music has its own volume; dual-GPU laptops draw on the discrete GPU.
- **Tier 2, reach:** a machine without Vulkan or DX12 plays through wgpu's GL backend; one switch sets a low-spec preset and switching it off restores the player's settings exactly; macOS has builds; the Windows build is signed (as Sarah decides); tools that drive Slippi Dolphin's playback (slp2mp4 and its kind) can drive melee-hd; `.slpz` files read and write; a replay can be paused, stepped, sought and rewound; the Recordings tab searches and sorts.
- **Tier 3, presentation:** anisotropic filtering, SSAA and sharpening; a CRT filter and librashader presets, separate from the CRT-feel delay; a sound effect starting one mix block sooner; ASIO output (if design call 4 keeps it); the adapter's polling rate shown; an external probe measuring a button press to its frame presented; NKit disc images play (moved from tier 1, Sarah 2026-11-27).
- **The rulings:** Slippi Dolphin's screen shake toggle and PAL stock icons as cosmetic toggles beside widescreen's; an update notice that links a newer GitHub release and downloads nothing; the settings panel localised if it stays simple (low priority).
- Every task's checks pass, the frame and mix checks are unchanged with every new option at its default, and each new piece of player-facing text is on Sarah's list.

**PhobGCC in software** is tier 1, beside rebinding: stock controllers on the adapter get PhobGCC's processing (calibration and notch remapping, the snapback filter, waveshaping, trigger modes, button remapping, per-controller profiles, and a diagnostic view). It's ported ("port it") with attribution: those files are GPL-3.0-only, in a module of their own that the rest of melee-hd only calls into, and README's license section then says that builds including it are distributed under GPL-3.0. It belongs in melee-hd because it changes only the inputs, as a Phob-modded pad does, and a `.slp` records the processed inputs.

**Rulings.** Slippi Dolphin's two optional cosmetic codes, PAL stock icons and no screen shake, are cosmetic toggles beside widescreen's ("allow both slippi codes"); no-shake touches camera state, so it needs the widescreen toggle's display-only proof ([design call 9](#design-calls) proposes building it so the state isn't touched at all). An update check is a notice linking a newer GitHub release, nothing downloaded ("update check notice is fine"). Localising the settings panel: "if it is simple enough but yeah low priority".

**Moved in.** Replay viewer controls were the netplay follow-on's ([the player](player.md#the-recordings-tab) still says "pausing, frame advance and rewinding are the netplay follow-on's"); they're melee-hd's now, since snapshots make them cheap. Task 15 updates that line.

**Out of melee-hd** (the follow-on repository, or dropped): stats, training tools, mods and imports, upscalers, DLSS and ray tracing, online play and bots, netplay.

**Standing rules this milestone keeps:**

- **Minimum latency is the default**; the CRT feel is opt-in ([the player](player.md#the-player), "the player minimizes latency rather than matching a CRT's").
- **The launcher is parked** (2026-10-28): the player boots into Melee and its front end is the settings panel. New settings live in that panel and, for a controller, in Melee's Options menu, never a launcher.
- **Player-facing text is used and listed** for Sarah's later sign-off, never blocking. Each task below names its text.
- **Gameplay-exact:** nothing here may change what the simulation does with a given input. Every feature is presentation, host-side input processing before the read the game takes, or file handling around the simulation; where one sits near the state, its check says how that's proved.

## The survey's findings that bear on the tiers

What reading the code and the upstream projects turned up that changes a task's size or shape from the tier list:

- **NKit isn't only a format to read.** nod (2.0.0-alpha.9, `crates/melee-data`'s reader) reads NKit 2 lossless inside CISO and WBFS, but not the `.nkit.iso` that GameCube NKit images usually are. And reading the files isn't enough: the fast disc speed times each read by its disc offset (`melee_sim::audio::clock::dolphin_read_cycles`, counted in 0x8000-byte blocks of the offset), and `Disc::read_after` hands the game the disc's bytes past a file's end. An image whose files sit elsewhere, or whose junk was removed, gives other load lengths and other leftover bytes than the retail disc, so another Melee. Support means restoring the retail layout and checking it against the retail image. M, not S (task 8).
- **PhobScope isn't PhobGCC's.** PhobGCC's upstream (below) has no PhobScope; its diagnostic is PhobVision, the PhobGCC 2 board's composite video menu (stick map plots, the snapback graph, the trigger view, button timing; `PhobGCC/rp2040/src/drawMenu.cpp`, `graph.cpp`, `drawGraphics.cpp`, the user guide in PhobGCC-doc's `For_Users/Phobvision_Guide_Latest.md`). Task 2's diagnostic ports those views into the settings panel.
- **PhobGCC on the adapter works on less than PhobGCC does.** The firmware reads the sticks' sensors at 12 bits and runs its filters once a millisecond (`readSticks` in `common/phobGCC.h`); through the adapter melee-hd gets the stock controller's 8-bit values once a report (125 a second on a stock adapter, about 1000 overclocked). Calibration, the notch remapping, the trigger modes and the remapping port as they are; the snapback filter and waveshaping step once per report with that report's time step, and do less at 125 Hz. They must run on the adapter's thread for every report (`crates/melee-input/src/adapter.rs`), not once a tick, or the filters see an eighth of the samples.
- **The adapter can't tell controllers apart.** Its report carries no serial, so "per-controller profiles" are named profiles assigned to the adapter's ports ([design call 14](#design-calls)).
- **PhobGCC's license.** PhobGCC-SW (github.com/PhobGCC/PhobGCC-SW, C++, last release v0.31 of 2025-11-16, `main` at `b4f175ea`) has the GPL version 3 text as its `LICENSE` and GitHub reports GPL-3.0; its source files carry no headers and nothing grants "or later", so its ported files are GPL-3.0-only, as decided. HayBox (github.com/JonnyHaystack/HayBox), whose Melee mode a box layout would follow, is the same: the GPL version 3 text, no "or later" ([design call 13](#design-calls)).
- **ASIO's licensing is settled.** Steinberg has offered the ASIO SDK under GPLv3 beside its proprietary license since 2025-10-15, so melee-hd's GPL-3.0-or-later code can use it. What's left is whether it's worth the build's cost ([design call 4](#design-calls)).
- **Melee has a sound balance, but it never lowers the music.** Melee's Options' sound balance (`gm_801603B0`, `balance_voice` and `balance_fx` in `crates/melee-sim/src/lb/audio/boot.rs`) lowers the announcer toward one end and the effects toward the other, and the music stays full. It's kept on the memory card, so under the Slippi ruleset, which has no card, it's the default at every power-on. A music volume belongs in the mixer, outside the simulation (task 6).
- **PAL stock icons isn't one of Slippi Dolphin's optional codes.** The installed Slippi Dolphin's `GALE01r2.ini` (playback and netplay) offers "Disable Screen Shake" as an optional code, and no PAL stock icons code; PAL stock icons is part of Slippi's console PAL injection list (`Sys/Slippi/InjectionLists/list_console_PAL.json`, `External/PAL/Core/PAL Stock Icons.asm` at 0x802F9A3C, from slippi-ssbm-asm). Disable Screen Shake makes `Camera_RequestQuake` (0x80030E44, `game::camera::request_quake`) return at once, which also skips the stage's quake model (`grlib::quake_model`, an object the stage makes for the quake) and the camera's quake counters and log, all simulation state. So in the simulation, no-shake wouldn't be cosmetic ([design call 9](#design-calls)).
- **macOS releases meet "PGO mandatory".** Release assets are profile-guided, never a plain fallback (Sarah, 2026-11-21). A macOS profile has to be trained on a Mac with the disc, and GitHub's runners never have the disc ([design call 6](#design-calls)).
- **Seeking a replay is harder than restoring a snapshot.** The simulation's state is a `clone` (1.45 MB, 0.14 ms), but the frame builder (`melee_render::frame::Frames`) and the mixer (`melee_audio::Mixer`, its 16 MB ARAM image and the effects) carry state from frame to frame and aren't `Clone`. [The player](player.md#the-recordings-tab) already notes that a new frame builder mid-session draws wrong. Task 15 starts with that.
- **Ports follow device order, so they move.** `melee_input::Inputs::read` gives the adapter its own ports, then gamepads in gilrs's order, then the keyboard (with the first gamepad if there is one). A gamepad unplugged mid-match moves every later device to another port. Task 1 fixes that as part of assignment.
- **gilrs puts a deadzone on every gamepad.** `gilrs::Gilrs::new` turns on gilrs's default filters, a deadzone and jitter filter, and `gamepad` in `melee-input` scales -1 to 1 to ±127 with truncation. A box over DInput needs its own path, raw and exact (task 3).
- **The GL fallback has hard requirements to check first.** melee-gpu asks for `DUAL_SOURCE_BLENDING` and `wgpu::Limits::default()` (`crates/melee-gpu/src/lib.rs`, `Renderer::with_adapter`), and the exact renderer's vertex shader reads each vertex's words from a storage buffer. A GL device without those can't run them as they are (task 9).
- **Searching recordings needs an index.** The Recordings tab reads each file only when its row shows, the first 4 KB and the metadata at its end, so a OneDrive folder downloads only what's seen (`crates/melee-player/src/recordings.rs`). A search over thousands has to read every file's summary once and keep it (task 16).

## What exists today

### Input

`crates/melee-input` reads three kinds of device into the four ports' `PADStatus`, just before each tick (`Inputs::read`):

- **The adapter** (`adapter.rs`): read raw over USB with nusb on a thread of its own, the latest report kept, each port's report turned into what `PADRead` returns, with the origin calibration the console's SI and PAD library do (`status`, `clamp_s8`, the X patch) and X+Y+Start held three seconds to take the origin again. Rumble from the game's motor commands. `Adapter::reports` counts reports, which the latency log turns into a rate; nothing shows it in the panel.
- **Gamepads** (`gamepad` in `lib.rs`): gilrs, with a fixed Xbox-style mapping (South A, West B, East X, North Y, right bumper Z, the triggers' travel as L and R with the digital click past 0.95), the sticks scaled to ±127, gilrs's deadzone on.
- **The keyboard** (`Keyboard`, the window's key codes mapped in `main.rs`'s key table: arrows, IJKL, X Z C S for A B X Y, D for Z, Q W for L R, Enter for Start, TFGH for the D-pad, left Shift halving the stick). Left and right together give neither (no SOCD resolution), and it plays with the first gamepad when there is one.

Nothing is rebindable and no device is assigned: ports follow device order. The session recording takes the reads the game took (`crate::session_rec`), after all of this, so whatever the input layer does is recorded and plays back the same in melee-hd and ssbm-rs. `--added-delay` (`melee_input::Delay`) sits after it. The gates (`crate::gates`) show how the player can act on the game's menus without changing game code: they read the main menu's cursor from the state and hold buttons back.

### The settings panel

`crate::front` (egui, drawn on the player's own window, `crate::ui`): three tabs, Settings (Display, Latency, Audio, Game, Save, Recording, Developer), Quick match and Recordings ([the player](player.md#the-player)). Esc opens it over the paused game and closes it; nothing else does. egui has keyboard focus navigation, but nothing feeds it a controller. Settings are kept as `key = value` lines in `settings.txt` (`Settings::save`, `Settings::restore`, `SETTINGS_VERSION` 2), and `--settings-shot` draws the panel to a PNG without a window, which is how a new section is checked by eye.

Melee's Options list (`think_settings`, `mn_8022D104`, in `crates/melee-sim/src/game/mn/main.rs`): six entries, confirm opening Rumble, Sound, the screen display, Language or Erase Data, and its fourth slot doing nothing on confirm.

### Audio

`melee_audio::Mixer` runs the AX microcode over each audio frame's parameter blocks and the aux effects after it; `Output` plays the samples (WASAPI on Windows, shared at the engine's smallest period or exclusive, cpal elsewhere), with one Volume (the square of its fraction, applied as the device takes samples). The music is the synth's stream (`melee_sim::audio::stream`, one or two voices playing an `.hps` file's boxes), mixed with everything else by the microcode. The mix is sample-exact with ssbm-rs's on the matches checked (`tools/audio-check`).

### Presentation and pacing

Two renderers (Fast by default, Exact the reference), MSAA 4x, any internal scale, any aspect ratio, High refresh (Extrapolate) on by default, just-in-time ticks ([the player](player.md#just-in-time-ticks)). On Windows the refresh times come from the compositor (`DwmGetCompositionTimingInfo`, `crate::timing`); whether the display is variable-refresh is guessed from the rate (`auto` takes a rate that isn't a multiple of 60 for VRR), and when a frame reached the screen isn't known: the open issue "Just-in-time ticks, what's left" ([the player](player.md#open-issues)) lists DXGI's frame statistics, `VK_KHR_present_wait`, `VK_GOOGLE_display_timing`, Wayland's presentation-time protocol and `CAMetalDisplayLink` as the sources. Linux has no refresh times at all yet. The adapter's request asks for `PowerPreference::HighPerformance` (`present.rs`, `melee-gpu`), but the executable exports nothing for a laptop's driver to read. No texture filtering beyond what GX asks for, no post-processing pass but the present's scaling.

### Discs

`melee_data::disc::Disc` opens any image nod reads (ISO, RVZ, WIA, CISO, GCZ, WBFS, TGC) and refuses anything but GALE01 revision 2 by its header. There's no hash check: a modified or partly scrubbed image with the right header opens.

### Replays and recordings

`--replay` and the Recordings tab play a `.slp` from start to finish, Melee set aside meanwhile and coming back where it was (`main.rs`'s `Aside`). No pause, step, seek or rewind. The tab lists a folder three deep, newest first, read lazily, with a fixed order and no search. Replays are `.slp` only. There's no Dolphin-style command line: `--replay FILE` and `--bench` are melee-hd's own.

### Platforms and releases

CI builds and tests on Linux, Windows and macOS (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`). Releases carry the Windows exe (PGO-built on this PC by the coordinator) and the Linux archive (`release.yml`, Ubuntu 22.04, from a profile trained on the worker). No macOS asset, nothing signed, no update check: the window's title names the build (`crate::channel`). On macOS the settings folder falls back to `~/.config/melee-hd` (`settings::folder_of`), not Apple's `~/Library/Application Support`.

## Proposed tasks

Twenty-five tasks in three waves. Sizes: S is a module or a few hundred lines with its tests, M a few files across one or two crates, L a new subsystem or a port of a few thousand lines. Every task keeps every frame and mix check passing with its options at their defaults (`cargo test --release -p melee-render-check -p melee-audio-check -- --ignored`, run on the worker where the brief says), adds unit tests for what it adds, mutation-tests each new check, and lists its player-facing text verbatim in its report. Where a task's code runs near the simulation's state, its check plays replays or sessions with the feature on and off and finds every tick's state equal (`StateEq`, as the sync test and `cosmetic_tests` compare it).

**Beside task 34** (the kinds' fields as `c_enum!` types, which wants a quiet melee-sim): every task below but 23 leaves melee-sim's structs alone. Tasks 4 and 6 read the state through existing fields and may add a function to melee-sim, never a field. Task 23 waits for task 34 only if design call 9 puts no-shake in the simulation.

**Beside the prediction thread** (Extrapolate's prediction on its own thread, the fast renderer's track: `main.rs`'s frame loop, `paced.rs`, `power.rs`'s predictor): the tasks that change the frame loop's pacing wait until it merges: 5 (presentation feedback, `present.rs`, `jit.rs`, `timing.rs`, `pacing.rs`), 13 (the hidden window and the frame dumps run the loop unpaced) and 15 (a replay's pause and seek change when its ticks run). 9 (the GL fallback) touches `present.rs`'s surface and device setup and goes after 5. The rest don't touch the loop.
