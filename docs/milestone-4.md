# Milestone 4: items, every stage, the CPU AI

A survey of what milestone 4 needs and a plan for splitting it into small agent tasks, each checkable on its own. The milestone is done when `melee-oracle diff` runs clean on every local replay whose content is ported, ssbm-rs's per-tick `STATE_TRACE` diffs are clean for every non-VS mode and menu, and leaf-check covers every leaf. Surveyed on 2026-10-16 from `main` at `91b0794f`; the counts are that day's.

## What ssbm-rs can play without a replay

ssbm-rs's runner (`tools/run` in ssbm-rs) can start a match three ways that don't need a recording:

- **Gecko-coded matches** (`BENCH_CODES=NAME.ini`): the codes boot into the debug VS result screen and hand `StartMelee` a whole `StartMeleeData` and seed, the way Slippi's playback starts a replay. `BENCH_AI=PORTS` has the level 9 CPU AI play those ports through their controllers (the random seed put back after it decides), so the match replays as human inputs; `BENCH_SLP=OUT.slp` records it with Slippi's recording codes, and `STATE_TRACE` works in the same run. `melee-bench codes` and `oracle-codes` (`tools/bench/src/matches.rs`) write the codes for the benchmark and oracle matches, which are traced from their recordings with `--replay`. ssbm-rs takes any start data, so items on, up to four players, real CPU slots at any level and any VS stage need only a richer generator: today's has a fixed rules block (items off, 4 stocks, 8 minutes), two players and level 9 player blocks.
- **Debug VS random matches** (`--mode e --matches SEED`): a stage from the 29 VS stages (`MATCH_STAGES`), fighters (`MATCH_FIGHTERS`, bosses included), up to four ports each a CPU half the time at a random level, the item switch (`MATCH_ITEMS`, frequency none 15% of the time) and rules (`MATCH_TIME`, `MATCH_TEAMS`, `MATCH_STAMINA`, `MATCH_CPU_KIND`), humans played by `--monkey`. No Slippi recording, so no `diff`; it is where leaf-check's `debug*` capture sets come from.
- **Other modes and the menus:** `--mode` takes the game mode (hex: 3 Classic, 4 Adventure, 5 All-Star, f Target Test, 1c Training, 20 Home-Run Contest, 21 and 22 Multi-Man, 2b Event, among others) with `EVENT`, `CLASSIC_STAGE`, `ADVENTURE_STAGE` and friends; `--monkey` and `MONKEY_HOLD` drive the menus; `--record FILE` keeps every controller read of a session and `--inputs FILE` plays it back headless (ssbm-rs's `inputs.rs`, format `SSBMINP1`).

ssbm-rs itself is trustworthy in these areas: its round rf (2026-10-14) verified 97.6% of item blocks, 95.0% of stage blocks and 97.1% of fighter blocks by lockstep against the original, 93.5% of game modes and 95.1% of menus. Its weakest stages are the trophy collector (78%), `grmaterial` (87%), Mushroom Kingdom (88%) and Kongo Jungle N64 (89%); a difference melee-hd finds there may be ssbm-rs's, which the porting guide says to stop and ask about.

### Oracle per area

**Items.** Items-on oracle matches from the generator: two ported characters as `BENCH_AI` players on Final Destination or Battlefield (stage code already ported), a time match of about two minutes, item frequency high and the item switch narrowed to one family of kinds, so each match exercises few kinds and a stop names its family. The level 9 AI picks items up, throws, swings and uses them, so the fighters' item states get exercised too. A two-minute items match traces to about 315 MB with memory (task 1's pilot; the three playback traces above, which end earlier, are 144 to 182 MB). The Poké Ball can't choose its Pokémon, so its matches use several seeds until every Pokémon has come out. Each match is recorded, replay-checked by ssbm-rs both ways (the playback and `BENCH_PADS`), and traced from its recording as the benchmark matches are; `melee-oracle diff` then runs it like any replay.

**Stages.** One oracle match per stage, items off, two ported characters as `BENCH_AI` players, long enough for the stage's cycle: four minutes for most, eight for the moving stages (Rainbow Cruise, Big Blue, Mute City, Poké Floats, Icicle Mountain), whose courses take minutes to come round. About 600 MB of trace per four minutes (2.6 MB a second in the pilots). Keep the Ice Climbers out of these matches until the CPU AI is ported: Nana runs the AI, which stops on several stages (Rainbow Cruise and Icicle Mountain, `cpu_ai.rs`, `cpu_move.rs`; Hyrule Temple's search).

**The CPU AI.** Real CPU slots, not `BENCH_AI`: the generator writes a CPU player block (slot type, computer kind and level), ssbm-rs plays the AI and records the match. Slippi records a CPU's inputs and playback injects them over what the AI asked for (`playback_inject`, the code at `0x8006B0DC`), but the AI still runs every tick and its state, the CPU block with what it asked for included, is in the fighter the diff compares. So a real-CPU recording traced with `--replay` checks every decision tick by tick, though a wrong decision doesn't change the fighter's motion. A stronger check plays the CPU free: a trace in which nothing injects the CPU's inputs, and a `diff` that leaves CPU ports uninjected, so melee-hd's AI drives them. The recording run itself (the `BENCH_CODES` run with `STATE_TRACE`) doesn't serve as it stands: task 1's pilots showed its heap laid out differently from playback's (the fighters `0x2720` bytes lower; [the oracle](oracle.md#oracle-sets)). The player needs that before it offers CPU opponents. The three level 1 replays above are the first real-CPU oracles and need no generator.

**Modes and menus.** `diff` needs a replay today. A session recorded with `--record` (the controller reads from boot, the card beside it) is the oracle for the boot, the menus and every single-player mode: melee-hd would boot, take the same reads and compare each tick with ssbm-rs's trace of the `--inputs` run. That harness is the first task of the modes plan (below), not of this one.

### Leaf captures and the debug heap fill

Leaf captures come from ssbm-rs's lockstep runs (`CAPTURE`, `CAPTURE_FUNCS`): replays (`--replay … --lockstep`, the `hf-*` sets) import cleanly, but in debug VS runs (`--mode e`, the `debug*` sets) items sit in heap blocks filled with `0x10101010`, which `melee-capture`'s importer doesn't carry over. The importer follows a stale item's handle words only when they point into main memory, and treats an out-of-range word as dangling only for a few handle kinds (`Importer::import_handle`, `tools/capture/src/import.rs`); every other kind lands in `unhandled`. So any call with a live or new item differs, and the character checks take only functions whose states hold no item from those sets. Neither repository said where the fill comes from; the engine notes say debug VS captures hold a previous scene's bytes even in a session's first match, which Slippi's playback zeroes. Three fixes, cheapest first (all three are done, see task 2):

1. Capture items from Slippi-layout runs: play the generator's items-on recordings with `--replay … --lockstep` and `CAPTURE`, the way the character sets are made. No importer change, and it covers every item kind the oracle matches spawn.
2. Make the importer treat any out-of-range word in a stale item block as unknown until written, for every handle kind, so a call that never reads it compares clean and one that does stops with a stale read ("unread values are free", the rule the trace diffs already use for item variables).
3. Confirm the fill's source with `FILL_ARENA=00` and `FILL_ARENA=55` on one short debug VS capture run, so the importer change rests on a known cause.

Done on 2026-10-16 ([the oracle](oracle.md), "Debug VS heaps"): the fill is the opening movie's first frame, whose luma plane (16 in every byte) the boot's two fields of `MvOpen.mth` decode into a heap buffer before ssbm-rs's runner switches to the debug VS mode; `FILL_ARENA=00` and `55` leave it unchanged. The importer now brings in the blocks the item pool grows into with their bytes and makes a stale item word outside main memory unknown until written, of every handle kind, and an items-on debug VS set (`calls/items/debug1`) and a Mr. Saturn and Beam Sword one (`calls/pulls/debug1`) run in `items` and `pulls`, as do item and pull sets captured from task 1's items-on pilot (`oracle-items-ported-fd`), fix 1.

## Proposed tasks

Fifteen tasks in four waves. Sizes count functions to port or check: S under 100 (or tooling), M 100 to 300, L over 300. Every porting task follows the porting guide: each function gets a leaf-check or a line in `ledger/checks.txt`, the oracle diffs it names run clean with no stale read and with `--heap`, `sync-test --rollback 7` passes on them, and each new check is mutation-tested. Oracle matches are made with the task 1 generator and traced into `local/traces/v5-mixed/` (names-off, `--port all`, as the others are); captures come from those recordings (task 2).

**Wave 1** (tasks 1 and 2 at once; both small)

1. **Oracle match generator.** Extend `tools/bench/src/matches.rs` so a match sets the item frequency and switch mask, the time or stock rules, two to four players, each a human (for `BENCH_AI`) or a real CPU with its computer kind and level, and any VS stage, and add the milestone's match sets (items by family, stages, CPUs) with a `melee-bench` subcommand that writes their codes. Make two pilots, an items-on `BENCH_AI` match on Final Destination and a real level 9 CPU match on Battlefield, and run ssbm-rs's replay check on each (the CPU one shows whether playback reproduces a real-CPU recording). Oracle: ssbm-rs's replay checks clean, both ways for the `BENCH_AI` pilot. Size: S (tooling, under an hour of ssbm-rs). Depends on nothing. **Done (2026-10-16, `feat/m4-generator`):** `melee-bench oracle-sets` and `tools/bench/make-oracle.sh` ([the oracle](oracle.md#oracle-sets)), 69 matches in three sets (18 items, 23 stages, 28 CPU; the CPU level sweep is three four-player matches rather than nine).
